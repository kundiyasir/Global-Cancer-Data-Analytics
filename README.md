# Global-Cancer-Data-Analytics
This project performs exploratory, inferential, and predictive analysis on a large-scale global cancer dataset to uncover insights into risk factors, treatment costs, survival outcomes, and healthcare disparities across countries and demographics.  Dataset includes 50,000 cancer patient records collected between 2015–2024 from multiple countries.

Objectives
Identify patterns in cancer diagnosis, severity, and survival outcomes
Analyze relationships between genetic, lifestyle, environmental, and economic factors
Examine healthcare cost disparities across demographics and regions
Generate insights to support healthcare policy and decision-making

Data Description
Demographics: Age, gender, country, diagnosis year
Risk Factors: Genetic predisposition, smoking, alcohol use, obesity
Environmental Exposure: Air pollution
Clinical Variables: Cancer type, stage, severity score
Economic Variables: Treatment cost
Outcomes: Survival years

Tools & Techniques
Python (pandas, NumPy, matplotlib, seaborn)
Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
Inferential Statistics
Correlation & hypothesis testing
Feature relationship analysis

Key Analyses
Risk factors vs cancer severity and survival
Early vs late-stage diagnosis patterns
Treatment cost vs survival outcomes
Impact of genetic risk combined with lifestyle factors
Cross-country and age-group disparities

Key Insights
Higher cancer stages are associated with increased treatment costs and reduced survival
Lifestyle risks amplify severity, especially when combined with genetic predisposition
Significant disparities observed in diagnosis stage and treatment burden across regions

Real-World Impact
Findings can support:
Public health policy design
Resource allocation strategies
Preventive healthcare interventions
